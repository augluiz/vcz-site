Beyond translation

A translated site is still
a foreign site.

Most companies arriving in Brazil translate the existing site and consider the job done. The Portuguese may be perfect and the page still reads as something written for somebody else — because the conventions differ, not just the words. These are the six gaps that do the most damage.

01

Mobile is not the secondary case

Brazilian audiences lean on the phone far more heavily than most European teams plan for. A layout designed on a desktop and merely made to fit a small screen loses the majority of the visit. Here, the phone is the design brief, not an adaptation of it.

02

WhatsApp is a business channel

In Brazil it is a normal, expected way to reach a company — including between businesses. A site that offers only a contact form and an email address reads as distant and slow, and quietly loses enquiries that would have happened in a message.

03

Payment language is local

Brazilian buyers expect to see payment and commercial terms described the way they are used locally. Lifting the European wording across leaves the visitor unsure whether they can actually buy — and uncertainty at that moment is where carts and enquiries are abandoned.

04

Forms reject Brazilian data

Fields built around European identifiers, postcodes and phone formats silently refuse valid Brazilian input. The visitor does not report the bug — they leave. This is among the most common and most invisible causes of a form that "gets no submissions".

05

Trust is proven differently

A Brazilian B2B buyer looks for who already buys from you here, a reachable local contact, delivery terms and technical detail. Awards and head-office photography carry much less weight than a visible Brazilian reference does.

06

It is Brazilian Portuguese, specifically

Brazilian and European Portuguese differ enough in vocabulary and register that using the wrong one is noticeable in the first paragraph. Machine translation from English tends to land somewhere between the two, which reads as neither.

What we build

Six kinds of
web work.

Not every project needs a new website. Often the right answer is a focused fix to what already exists — and a first conversation is enough to tell which.

01

Brazilian-facing company site

A site written and structured for Brazilian visitors, rather than a translated copy of the European one. Fast, mobile-first, with the technical SEO groundwork in place from day one.

02

Product catalogue

For manufacturers and distributors: a portfolio buyers can browse by category, with search and filters — replacing the eighty-page PDF that is out of date the week it is sent.

03

Landing page for a campaign

One page, one objective: capture enquiries, support a launch or present a single offer to the Brazilian market, without a menu offering five ways to leave.

04

Localisation of an existing site

Adapting the site you already have: language, contact conventions, payment wording, form fields and the trust signals Brazilian buyers look for. Usually the cheapest meaningful improvement.

05

E-commerce and catalogue structure

Commercial review of the store for Brazilian shoppers: category tree, product page, search, filters and the path to checkout — delivered as changes your team implements, or implemented with you.

06

Web dashboards and data interfaces

Interactive data panels published on the web, for your team or your customers. This is our strongest technical ground — the public examples are below.
